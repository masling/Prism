## What Is a Prism?

An optical prism is a transparent element with angled flat surfaces that refract light. A dispersive prism can separate white light into colors because different wavelengths bend by different amounts. In ordinary glass, blue and violet light generally refract more strongly than red light.

Prism Riddle turns that physical idea into a puzzle. A prism may need to redirect a beam, spread its colors far enough for a filter, or send different wavelengths toward separate targets.

## Different Light Sources Create Different Puzzles

Light sources are part of the level design rather than fixed decoration. A chamber can change:

| Source property | Puzzle effect |
| --- | --- |
| Position and direction | Changes where the route begins and which openings are reachable |
| Narrow beam | Rewards precise angles and can pass through small openings |
| Wide beam | Requires lenses, apertures, or complete mirror coverage to avoid clipping |
| Angular spread | Creates a fan of light that grows wider over distance |
| Color | Limits which wavelengths are available and whether another source is needed |
| Energy | Makes splitting, filtering, and optical losses part of the budget |
| Multiple sources | Enables color mixing, opposing routes, and synchronized target conditions |

The free optical lab allows players to experiment with source color, beam width, energy, and angular spread alongside the more demanding combinations in the expanded campaign.

## Multiple Gates and Target Conditions

A chamber may end at one gate or several gates. Later Prism Riddle levels require several target conditions, including up to five lit gates and protected dark receivers to be satisfied at the same time.

Targets can require:

- A minimum amount of matching light
- Red, green, blue, white, or mixed light
- A required color purity
- A balance of two colors from different sources
- Another nearby target to remain completely dark

This creates branching and merging puzzles. One source may need to be divided among several gates, while two colored sources may need to meet on the same optical path.

## The Optical Toolkit

### Mirrors

Mirrors reflect only the part of a finite-width beam that reaches their surface. Their positions decide how much light is captured, while their angles control every later section of the route.

### Convex and Concave Lenses

Convex lenses gather light toward a focus. Concave lenses spread parallel light or modify a beam that is already converging. Lens position matters because the useful focus must occur where the chamber needs it.

### Prisms

Prisms refract light at both glass surfaces. Different wavelengths leave at different angles, turning color separation into spatial geometry.

### Beam Splitters

A splitter creates reflected and transmitted branches. It divides the incoming energy rather than giving both branches a full-strength copy.

### Color Filters

A filter passes one wavelength band and absorbs most others. It can select a color that already exists in the beam, but it cannot create a missing color.

### Apertures and Light Stops

An aperture allows only part of a beam to pass. A light stop absorbs the beam. Both are useful when one gate must be illuminated while another region remains dark.

### Dichroic Mirrors and Neutral Density Filters

Dichroic mirrors pass the marked color and reflect the other bands, creating separate color paths without duplicating energy. Neutral density filters reduce all wavelengths equally so sensitive receivers stay below their total-energy limit. Unequal splitters add 30/70, 40/60 and 60/40 branch budgets.

## How to Play

1. Find the lamp and follow its beam to the first obstacle.
2. Choose the next opening through the chamber.
3. Move an optical instrument fully into the beam.
4. Rotate it until the light reaches the next part of the route.
5. Check every gate’s energy and color readings.
6. Refine the route one section at a time.

The game checks the resulting light rather than comparing the layout with one fixed diagram. Every supplied instrument must carry light in formal chambers, but more than one precise arrangement may work.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Prism Riddle free?

The first three complete chambers and the optical lab are free. Chambers 4–80 are included in the one-time full-game purchase.

### Is Prism Riddle a Prism Rules game?

No. Prism Riddle is an independent light-bending game. It is not an official version, sequel, download, or APK of Prism Rules.

### Does the game use real optical behavior?

The simulation uses finite-width geometric light sampled across 27 wavelengths. It includes reflection, wavelength-dependent refraction, thin-lens focusing, beam splitting, filtering, and occlusion. It does not claim to simulate diffraction, interference, or polarization.

### Can one level have several light sources?

Yes. Sources can enter from different positions and directions and can use different colors, widths, energy, and angular spread.

### Can one level have several final targets?

Yes. Current advanced chambers include up to five simultaneous lit gates and multiple protected dark targets. Some gates must be lit while other marked targets must stay dark.

### Is progress saved?

Layouts, completed chambers, and personal-best times are saved in the current browser. Players with full access can generate a recovery code for another browser.

### Are exact walkthroughs included in the free hints?

Ordinary hints explain routing and optical conditions without publishing exact solution coordinates. Detailed walkthroughs are available as optional purchases. Every chamber requires an explicit unlock confirmation, including the one free unlock included with a full-game purchase. Cancelling spends nothing; viewing an already unlocked guide does not spend again. Purchases and unlocks are restored together with the purchase recovery code.
